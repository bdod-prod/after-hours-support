Replies with a useful next step

These conversations and the business behind them are fictional. The example repair shop is staffed Monday–Friday, 09:00–18:00, and Saturday, 10:00–16:00. Its chatbot has help content, but no live job or booking data.

A question the content can answer

Source: the shop's current opening-hours page.

Visitor“Are you open on Sunday?”

“The shop is closed on Sunday. It opens on Monday at 09:00.”

The answer gives the relevant hours. There is no reason to turn a simple question into a callback request.

A question that needs a record

Source: the contact process. The chatbot cannot inspect repair jobs.

Visitor“Is my bike ready to collect?”

“I can't see individual repair jobs. Use the contact page to send your job reference and preferred contact details. The team reads requests during the shop's staffed hours.”

This gives a route to the people who can check. It makes no claim that the bike is ready or that a request has already been sent.

A request that needs an action

Source: the booking policy. Changes need confirmation from the team.

Visitor“Move my Monday appointment to Tuesday.”

“I can't change a booking here. Send the appointment details and requested date through the contact page. The team will check availability during staffed hours and confirm whether it can be moved.”

The existing appointment still stands until the team confirms a change. If the chatbot can submit a request, its reply should reflect whether that submission actually succeeded.

Give the next shift something it can use.

Agree the coverage

Write down the staffed hours and time zone, which inbox or form receives requests, and who checks it. Match any stated response window to what the team can deliver.

If nobody monitors the channel overnight, make that clear in the reply. Customers need to know whether they are asking someone who is available now.

Close the loop

At the next shift, the owner checks the request, confirms what still needs doing, and follows up. A useful record keeps the original question, the route used and the next action together.

Look for repeated gaps in the help content. Keep one-off account questions separate from information that could help the next visitor.

Write your coverage plan.

The editable template has space for hours, contact routes, answer boundaries and follow-up responsibilities. Fill it in with the people who actually pick up the requests.
